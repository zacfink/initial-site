# QWEB exec meeting — get ready

A script for Zac to deliver live to the co-chairs and exec team.
Speaking time is roughly 10 minutes, plus two pauses that will run longer.

**Fill in before you deliver this:** the meeting date, the date of the next
meeting, and the names against each area. Everything with a `[ ]` in front of
it is someone's homework, and the whole point of the meeting is that nobody
leaves without knowing which ones are theirs.

**A note on the areas below.** They are written by function, not by title. If
your exec titles split the work differently, move the items, but do not drop
them. Every one of these is something that will otherwise land on a co-chair
in week six.

---

## 1. Open

> I want to keep this short and I want everyone to leave with a list.
>
> The club works right now because a small number of us are carrying specific
> things in our heads. That was fine when we were small. It does not survive
> this term. We have more members, more sessions, and more people expecting
> something from us than we have ever had at once.
>
> So this meeting is not a check-in. It is us getting ready. By the end of it
> every person here owns an area out loud, has a written list for it, and has
> a date. Then we go hire directors, because none of us can run an area alone
> and I am not asking you to.

*(No pause here. Keep going.)*

---

## 2. The standard, said once

Say this once, plainly, and then never repeat it — the rest of the meeting
should demonstrate it instead of restating it.

> Owning an area means three things, and it is the same three for everybody.
>
> One: you are the person who gets asked. If someone has a question about your
> area, it comes to you and it stops with you.
>
> Two: you decide. You do not bring me the decision, you bring me the outcome.
> If it is genuinely above your line, say so early, not the day before.
>
> Three: when it slips, it is yours. Not to be blamed for. To fix, and to tell
> the rest of us about before we find out on our own.
>
> That is it. If you are doing tasks somebody hands you, you are not owning an
> area yet, and that is the thing changing today.

---

## 3. What every one of you does before the next meeting

> Same five items for everyone, regardless of area. This should take you an
> hour, not an evening.

- [ ] **Write your area in one sentence.** What you own, and what breaks if
      you do not do it. If you cannot finish the sentence, your area is not
      defined yet and we fix that this week, not in October.
- [ ] **List every recurring thing your area needs this term, with dates.**
      Not goals. The actual repeating work — the sessions, the posts, the
      emails, the bookings. Put it on a calendar the rest of us can see.
- [ ] **Name the two or three things only you can do.** Everything else on
      that list is a director's job. Mark them.
- [ ] **Write your director posting.** One paragraph. What the role does, what
      they will actually work on in the first month, and roughly how many
      hours a week you are asking for. Be honest about the hours.
- [ ] **Bring one number you are judged on.** One. Pick something you can
      actually count and that would embarrass you if it were flat all term.

> Send all five to the shared doc before the next meeting, not at it. If it
> arrives at the meeting we spend the meeting reading instead of deciding.

---

## 4. What each area does specifically

> I have written a starting list for each area so nobody has to invent one
> from scratch. Add to it. Do not quietly subtract from it.

### Events

- [ ] Term calendar locked: every session and event with a date, a time, and a
      room actually booked. Bookings first, promotion second.
- [ ] One flagship event named, dated, and scoped this week. The thing people
      will remember the term for.
- [ ] A run-of-show template every event uses: setup time, who opens, who
      handles the room, who closes, who cleans up.
- [ ] A named backup for every event you personally cannot attend.

### Curriculum and education

- [ ] The full session sequence for the term, in order, with a one-line
      description of what someone can do after each one.
- [ ] A named person teaching each session, confirmed by them, not assumed.
- [ ] Materials due one week before each session, not the night before. Put
      those due dates in the shared calendar as their own deadlines.
- [ ] A path for someone who shows up in week five having missed everything.
      We will have a lot of those and right now we lose them.

### Marketing and design

- [ ] A content calendar built off the events calendar, not separately from
      it. Every event gets a post before and a post after.
- [ ] Every brand asset in one folder that other execs can reach without
      asking you. Templates, logo files, slide deck, post sizes.
- [ ] A stated posting cadence, and someone who posts when you are busy.
- [ ] **The director recruitment campaign.** This one is urgent and it is
      yours: posts, story assets, and a single link, ready to go live the day
      applications open.

### Sponsorship and finance

- [ ] A term budget with real numbers, circulated to the exec, showing what
      each area can actually spend.
- [ ] A sponsor list with named contacts and their status, in one sheet
      everybody can see.
- [ ] The outreach deck finished and sent to the first ten targets. Companies
      set their budgets early and we have historically asked late.
- [ ] A written answer to "how do we reimburse someone", so execs stop paying
      for club things and hoping.

### Operations and internal

- [ ] One member list that is actually current, and one obvious way to join.
- [ ] Channels tidied: one channel per area, archived what is dead, and every
      exec added where they belong.
- [ ] A one-page onboarding doc for new directors, so their first week is not
      guesswork.
- [ ] Meeting cadence set for the term, with a named notetaker each week and
      notes posted within a day.

### Web and tech

- [ ] The site's owner named, and what is shipping to it this term listed.
- [ ] A written way for a director or member to contribute to the site without
      breaking it. This is one of the best recruiting tools we have and right
      now it does not exist.
- [ ] Access sorted: who holds the domain, the hosting, and the accounts, and
      a second person who also holds them.

*(**Pause here. This is the long one.** Go around the room. Every person says
out loud which area is theirs and one thing from their list they are doing
first. Do not move on until everyone has spoken. If two people claim the same
area, settle it now, in the room.)*

---

## 5. Directors — how we actually get them

> I am not asking any of you to run an area alone. Directors are how the
> ownership gets shared, and they are how we find the next exec team without
> guessing.
>
> This is not a title handout. A director gets a real piece of work, a real
> deadline, and your time. If you cannot describe what they will do in their
> first month, you are not ready to hire one, and the fix is item four on your
> homework list.
>
> Here is the timeline. It is tight on purpose. Applications that open in
> October get people who have already committed to something else.

- [ ] **This week —** every exec writes their director posting. One paragraph
      each. Marketing collects them into one page.
- [ ] **This week —** decide how many directors per area. Two is usually
      right. One is fragile and four is a committee.
- [ ] **Applications open [DATE] —** one form, one deadline, one link. Not a
      separate process per area. Ask for a short answer and something they
      have made, not a résumé.
- [ ] **Promotion push, [DATE] to [DATE] —** every exec personally messages
      three people they think would be good. The form catches the rest. The
      best directors almost never apply cold.
- [ ] **Applications close [DATE].** Hard close. We have extended before and
      it cost us two weeks.
- [ ] **Interviews, [DATE] to [DATE] —** you run your own. Fifteen minutes,
      three questions, same three for everybody. A co-chair sits in on the
      first two you run.
- [ ] **Offers out by [DATE] —** and this is the part we have historically
      been bad at: every director gets a real task within one week of saying
      yes. Not an onboarding call. A task. People who are not used in their
      first two weeks are gone by November.

> Your directors are your responsibility, not the co-chairs'. If one of them
> goes quiet, that is a conversation you have, and I want to hear about it
> early rather than at the end of term.

*(**Pause.** Take questions on the timeline specifically. The dates are the
thing people will push back on, and it is worth settling now rather than in
the group chat afterwards.)*

---

## 6. Close

> Before the next meeting, three things from every person here. Your five
> homework items in the shared doc. Your director posting written. Your first
> area item started, not planned.
>
> Next meeting we do not review the lists. We review what moved.
>
> One last thing. If your area is wrong, if it is too big, or if you have
> taken on something you cannot carry this term, tell me this week and tell me
> privately. That is not a failure and I would much rather hear it now than
> discover it in week eight. What I do not want is someone sitting on it.
>
> That is everything. Go write your lists.

---

## Delivery notes for Zac

- **Do not soften the open.** The first thirty seconds set whether this reads
  as a real change or as another meeting. Say it flat and move on.
- **The room-going-around in section 4 is the meeting.** Everything else is
  setup for it. Protect the time, and if you are running late, cut section 2
  rather than that pause.
- **Someone will say "can I own two areas."** Usually the answer is no, and
  the reason to give is that two areas means the second one gets whatever is
  left over. Directors are the answer to needing more hands.
- **Say the same three ownership rules to directors when they are hired.** The
  standard only holds if it is the same one all the way down.
- **Send the written version within an hour of the meeting.** Sections 3, 4,
  and 5 as a checklist, with names attached to areas as claimed in the room.
  Verbal commitments made in a meeting decay by the weekend.
