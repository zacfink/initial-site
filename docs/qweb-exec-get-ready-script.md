# QWEB exec meeting — get ready

For the co-chairs and the exec team. Meeting date: **[DATE]**.

**How to use this.** Sections 1, 2, 5 and 6 are written to be said out loud,
in Zac's voice, and run about ten minutes total plus the go-around. Sections 3
and 4 are not read aloud. They are the handout. Send them the moment the
meeting ends so every exec has their own list in writing.

**Fill in before you deliver it:** the meeting date, the next meeting date,
the six dates in the director timeline, and the names against each area.

**A note on the areas.** They are written by function, not by title. If your
exec titles split the work differently, move the items between areas, but do
not drop any. Everything here otherwise lands on a co-chair in week six.

---

## 1. Open

*Say this. Do not soften it, and do not pause for agreement.*

> I want to keep this short and I want everyone to leave with a list.
>
> The club works right now because a few of us are carrying specific things in
> our heads. That was fine when we were small. It does not survive this term.
> We have more members, more sessions, and more people expecting something
> from us than we have ever had at once.
>
> So this is not a check-in. This is us getting ready. By the end of it every
> person here owns an area out loud, has a written list for it, and has a
> date. Then we go hire directors, because none of you should be running an
> area alone and I am not asking you to.
>
> I am also going to tell you what the two of us are doing in the background,
> so this does not sound like we are handing out work and walking away.

---

## 2. For all of us

### The standard

*Say this once. The rest of the meeting should demonstrate it rather than
repeat it.*

> Owning an area means three things, and it is the same three for everybody.
>
> One: you are the person who gets asked. Questions about your area come to
> you and they stop with you.
>
> Two: you decide. You do not bring me the decision, you bring me the outcome.
> If it is genuinely above your line, say so early, not the day before.
>
> Three: when it slips, it is yours. Not to be blamed for. To fix, and to tell
> the rest of us before we find out on our own.
>
> If you are doing tasks somebody hands you, you are not owning an area yet,
> and that is the thing changing today.

### The five things every exec does before the next meeting

Same five for everyone, whatever your area. This is an hour of work, not an
evening.

- [ ] **Write your area in one sentence.** What you own, and what breaks if
      you do not do it. If you cannot finish the sentence, your area is not
      defined and we fix that this week.
- [ ] **Calendar every recurring thing your area needs this term.** Not goals.
      The actual repeating work: sessions, posts, emails, bookings. On the
      shared calendar where the rest of us can see it.
- [ ] **Mark the two or three things only you can do.** Everything else on
      that list is a director's job.
- [ ] **Write your director postings.** One paragraph per role. What they do,
      what they will actually work on in month one, and honest hours.
- [ ] **Bring one number you are judged on.** One. Something countable that
      would embarrass you if it were flat all term.

Send all five to the shared doc **before** the next meeting. If it arrives at
the meeting, we spend the meeting reading instead of deciding.

### Three rules we are all holding to

- **The shared calendar is the source of truth.** If it is not on there, it is
  not happening, and nobody is expected to know about it.
- **Bad news travels early.** A slip told on Monday is a problem. The same
  slip found on Friday is a mess.
- **Nothing important lives in one person's head or one person's account.**
  Every access, document, and process has a second holder.

## 3. Getting your team going

The same timeline applies to every area. It is tight on purpose. Applications
that open in late October get people who already committed to something else.

- [ ] **This week —** every exec writes their director postings. Marketing
      collects them onto one page.
- [ ] **This week —** confirm how many directors per area. Two is usually
      right. One is fragile and four is a committee.
- [ ] **Applications open [DATE] —** one form, one deadline, one link. Not a
      separate process per area. Ask for a short answer and something they
      have made. Do not ask for a résumé.
- [ ] **Promotion push, [DATE] to [DATE] —** every exec personally messages
      three people they think would be good. The form catches the rest. The
      best directors almost never apply cold.
- [ ] **Applications close [DATE].** Hard close. We have extended before and
      it cost us two weeks.
- [ ] **Interviews, [DATE] to [DATE] —** you run your own. Fifteen minutes,
      three questions, the same three for every candidate. A co-chair sits in
      on the first two you run.
- [ ] **Offers out by [DATE].**

### The week after they say yes

This is the part we have historically been worst at, so it is written down.

- [ ] **A real task within one week.** Not an onboarding call. A task with a
      deadline. People who are not used in their first two weeks are gone by
      November.
- [ ] **A first team meeting inside ten days.** Thirty minutes. Walk them
      through your area, your calendar, and what they own inside it.
- [ ] **Say the same three ownership rules to them** that you heard in section
      2. The standard only holds if it is the same one all the way down.
- [ ] **A standing check-in.** Fifteen minutes a week, or half an hour every
      two. Put it in the calendar now, not when something goes wrong.

Your directors are your responsibility, not the co-chairs'. If one goes quiet,
that is your conversation to have, and we want to hear about it early.

## 4. Your section

Find yours. This is your starting list, not your whole job. Add to it. Do not
quietly subtract from it.

### Events

**You own:** every event happening, on time, in a booked room, with a named
person running it.

- [ ] Term calendar locked. Every session and event has a date, a time, and a
      room actually booked. Bookings first, promotion second.
- [ ] One flagship event named, dated, and scoped this week. The thing people
      remember the term for.
- [ ] A run-of-show template every event uses: setup, who opens, who handles
      the room, who closes, who cleans up.
- [ ] A named backup for every event you cannot personally attend.

**Your team: 2 directors.**

- *Logistics* — bookings, room setup, supplies, day-of operations. **First
  task:** every room for the term booked, inside their first week.
- *Programming* — what actually happens at each event, speakers, activities.
  **First task:** scope the flagship and bring three options.

### Curriculum and education

**You own:** what a member can do by the end of term that they could not do at
the start.

- [ ] The full session sequence, in order, each with a one-line description of
      what someone can do after it.
- [ ] A named person teaching each session, confirmed by them, not assumed.
- [ ] Materials due one week before each session. Put those due dates in the
      shared calendar as their own deadlines.
- [ ] A path for someone who shows up in week five having missed everything.
      We will have a lot of those and right now we lose them.

**Your team: 2 to 3 directors.**

- *Curriculum* — writes and maintains the session material. **First task:**
  sessions one and two written in full.
- *Workshops* — runs the room during sessions and helps people who are stuck.
  **First task:** recruit and schedule helpers for the first three sessions.

### Marketing and design

**You own:** that people know we exist and know what is happening next.

- [ ] A content calendar built off the events calendar, not separately from
      it. Every event gets a post before and a post after.
- [ ] Every brand asset in one folder other execs can reach without asking
      you. Templates, logo files, slide deck, post sizes.
- [ ] A stated posting cadence, and someone who posts when you are busy.
- [ ] **The director recruitment campaign.** Urgent, and yours. Posts, story
      assets, and one link, live the day applications open.

**Your team: 2 directors.**

- *Design* — graphics, templates, brand consistency. **First task:** the
  director recruitment assets, this week.
- *Social and content* — posting, stories, photos at events. **First task:**
  build the content calendar and shoot the first event.

### Sponsorship and finance

**You own:** that we can pay for what we plan, and that we ask early enough to
get a yes.

- [ ] A term budget with real numbers, circulated to the exec, showing what
      each area can actually spend.
- [ ] A sponsor sheet with named contacts and their status, visible to
      everyone.
- [ ] The outreach deck finished and sent to the first ten targets. Companies
      set budgets early and we have historically asked late.
- [ ] A written answer to "how do I get reimbursed", so execs stop paying for
      club things and hoping.

**Your team: 2 directors.**

- *Corporate outreach* — the pipeline, the emails, the follow-ups. **First
  task:** send the first ten.
- *Finance* — budget tracking, reimbursements, receipts. **First task:** build
  the tracking sheet and the reimbursement form.

### Operations and internal

**You own:** that the club runs like an organization rather than a group chat.

- [ ] One member list that is actually current, and one obvious way to join.
- [ ] Channels tidied. One per area, dead ones archived, every exec added
      where they belong.
- [ ] A one-page onboarding doc for new directors, so their first week is not
      guesswork.
- [ ] Meeting cadence set for the term, a named notetaker each week, notes
      posted within a day.

**Your team: 1 to 2 directors.**

- *Community* — member experience, welcoming new people, retention. **First
  task:** rebuild the member list and message everyone who came to one event
  and never came back.
- *Internal ops* — notes, records, the shared drive, the calendar. **First
  task:** set up the drive structure and move everything scattered into it.

### Web and tech

**You own:** the site, the accounts, and the way a member contributes code.

- [ ] The site's owner named, and what is shipping to it this term listed.
- [ ] A written way for a director or member to contribute without breaking
      anything. This is one of the best recruiting tools we have and right now
      it does not exist.
- [ ] Access sorted. Who holds the domain, the hosting, and the accounts, and
      a second person who also holds them.
- [ ] The site reflects reality: current calendar, current exec list.

**Your team: 2 directors.**

- *Web* — ships to the site. **First task:** put the term calendar live.
- *Projects* — runs the member contribution pipeline and reviews what comes
  in. **First task:** open five starter issues a real member could take.

## 5. What the co-chairs are doing

*Say this out loud. It is what makes the rest of the meeting land as shared
work rather than delegation. Adjust it to what the two of you actually
handle.*

> You should know what we are carrying, because otherwise this meeting sounds
> like we handed out the work and left.

**Keeping the club allowed to exist.** Ratification, funding applications,
insurance, and the paperwork nobody sees. If that lapses, nothing else on
these lists can happen.

**Money at the top.** The account, signing authority, and approving spend
above the line. Below that line it is your budget and your call.

**Space at the institutional level.** The booking relationships, and being the
people who escalate when a room falls through the day before.

**Sponsors at the relationship level.** Introductions, the large asks, and
anything with a contract attached. The pipeline itself is sponsorship's.

**Partnerships.** Other clubs, hackathons, companies, and the department.

**Each of you.** A standing one-on-one, unblocking anything stuck above your
line, and being the ones who take it publicly when something goes wrong.

**The hiring machinery.** One form, one deadline, one shared page, so that you
spend your time interviewing rather than building process six separate times.

**Watching the seams.** Catching it when two areas book the same week or
promise the same people two different things.

**Next year.** Making sure this gets handed over rather than rediscovered from
scratch by whoever follows us.

> And what we are not doing, on purpose: we are not approving your decisions,
> we are not writing your lists, and we are not chasing your directors. If you
> find us doing any of those, something has gone wrong and we should talk
> about it.

---

## 6. Close

> Three things from every person before the next meeting. Your five items in
> the shared doc. Your director postings written. Your first area item
> started, not planned.
>
> Next meeting is **[DATE]** and we are not reviewing the lists. We are
> reviewing what moved.
>
> Last thing. If your area is wrong, if it is too big, or if you have taken on
> something you cannot carry this term, tell me this week and tell me
> privately. That is not a failure and I would much rather hear it now than
> find out in week eight. What I do not want is someone sitting on it.
>
> That is everything. Go write your lists.

## Delivery notes for Zac

- **Read sections 1, 2, 5 and 6.** Send sections 3 and 4 as the handout the
  moment the meeting ends. Verbal commitments decay by the weekend.
- **The go-around is the meeting.** After section 2, stop and have every
  person say out loud which area is theirs and the one thing they are doing
  first. Do not move on until everyone has spoken. If two people claim the
  same area, settle it in the room.
- **Second pause after section 3.** Take questions on the timeline
  specifically. The dates are what people will push back on and it is better
  settled there than in the group chat afterwards.
- **If you are running short on time, cut section 2's standard, not the
  go-around.** People can read the three rules. They cannot claim an area in
  writing with the same weight.
- **Someone will ask to own two areas.** The answer is usually no, and the
  reason is that the second one gets whatever is left over. Directors are the
  answer to needing more hands.
- **Send section 5 in writing too.** Execs repeat it to their own directors,
  and it is the part that stops "ownership" from sounding like offloading.
